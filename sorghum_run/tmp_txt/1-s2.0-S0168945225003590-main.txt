Salt stress is one of the main abiotic stresses that affects plant growth and development, as well as crop yield. A 
large number of studies have reported that the WRKY gene family plays significant roles in the plant responses to 
salt stress, but the underlying mechanisms remain largely unknown, and research on WRKY proteins in sorghum 
is also limited. In this study, we identified the sorghum gene SbWRKY51 , which encodes a group II WRKY 
transcription factor. The expression of SbWRKY51 was up-regulated by salt stress, drought, and abscisic acid 
(ABA) in sorghum. Overexpression of SbWRKY51 via Agrobacterium rhizogenes -mediated hairy root trans -
formation enhanced salt tolerance of sorghum with longer root length, increased biomass accumulation, less Na
content, and higher antioxidant enzyme activities compared with wild-type (WT) plants. Ectopic expression of 
SbWRKY51 in Arabidopsis showed similar phenotypes, and mutation of the homologous gene AtWRKY11 
increased salt sensitivity of Arabidopsis . Under salt stress, the SbWRKY51 -overexpressed transgenic sorghum 
showed altered expression levels of genes related to oxidative stress and lignin biosynthesis, as well as increased 
lignin content. Thus, these results suggest that SbWRKY51 positively regulates salt tolerance in sorghum by 
affecting lignin biosynthesis.
1.Introduction
Soil salinization has become one of the major global environmental 
issues for the modern agricultural productivity, which affects the 
morphogenesis, growth, and development of plants, as well as the crop 
yield and quality (He et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024 ). Salt stress can 
initially cause osmotic stress to plants, which affects plant root water 
uptake and stomatal closure. High salinity in soil can also induce ionic 
stress resulted from the excess absorption of Naand Cl- in cells, which 
can result in ion toxicity and nutrient imbalance (Hasegawa et al., 2000; 
Zhu, 2003; Fang et al., 2021; He et al., 2024 ). Osmotic stress and ionic 
stress can cause the excess accumulation of reactive oxygen species 
(ROS) in plant cells, which leads to oxidative stress and can damage cell 
structure and metabolic process (Wang et al., 2009; Fang et al., 2021; He 
et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024 ). Plants have evolved multiple mecha -
nisms to adapt to salt stress, such as the increase of antioxidant enzyme 
activities. On the other hand, salt stress can activate stress response 
pathways (e.g., ROS signaling) and key metabolic pathways, promoting the synthesis of various secondary metabolites, including lignin, which 
is produced via the phenylpropanoid pathway. In addition, lignin is a 
component of the plant cell wall (Chun et al., 2019 ). Under salt stress, 
lignin accumulation leads to increased lignification of the root Casparian 
strip, which effectively blocks the apoplastic transport of Na⁺ and Cl⁻ to 
the shoots, thereby mitigating salt toxicity. Besides, regulating the 
expression of downstream genes via transcription factors is also a key 
mechanism for plants to response to salt stress (Song et al., 2020; He 
et al., 2024 ).
Many transcription factor families such as basic helix-loop helix 
(bHLH) (Song et al., 2021 ), myeloblastosis (MYB) (Scully et al., 2016 ), 
WRKY (Song et al., 2022 ), NAC (Zhang et al., 2016 ) have been reported 
to be involved in plant responses to abiotic stress. Among these, the 
WRKY family is one of the largest transcription factor families in plants 
with multiple roles. WRKY transcription factors are defined by the 
presence of one or two ~60-residue WRKY domains, each contains a 
conserved N-terminal WRKYGQK core sequence and a C-terminal 
zinc-finger motif (C2-HC or C2-H2) (Ülker and Somssich, 2004; Agarwal

WRKY domains and the pattern of zinc-finger motif, WRKY transcription 
factors are classified into three groups: group I, II, and III (Eulgem et al., 
2000; Jiang et al., 2017; Chen et al., 2018 ). Under abiotic stresses, the 
WRKY transcription factor can regulate the expression of downstream 
stress-responsive genes via binding to the W-box (TTGACC/T) within 
their promoters (Yamasaki et al., 2005; Huang et al., 2022 ).
Numerous studies have reported that WRKY transcription factors are 
playing important roles in various plants. For example, cotton 
GhWRKY17 affected plant salt and drought tolerance through regulating 
ABA signaling and ROS production (Yan et al., 2014 ). OsWRKY54 was 
reported to improve salt tolerance of rice by binding to the promoter of 
OsHKT1;5 (Huang et al., 2022 ). SbWRKY55 affected sorghum salt 
response through regulating ABA signaling (Song et al., 2022 ). Over-
expression of GhWRKY34 could enhance the salt tolerance of Arabidopsis 
transgenic plants (Zhou et al., 2015 ). Overexpression of SlWRKY8 
enhanced plant tolerance to drought and salt stresses in Solanum lyco-
persicum (Gao et al., 2019 ). Eggplant SmWRKY40 improved the salt 
tolerance of transgenic Arabidopsis (Zhang et al., 2024 ). Although so 
many WRKY transcription factors have been shown to be involved in 
plant responses to salt stress, research on WRKY genes in sorghum is still 
relatively limited.
Sorghum (Sorghum bicolor [L.] moench) is an important grain, forage 
and bioenergy crop and is also one of the widely cultivated crops 
globally (Mullet et al., 2014; Boyles et al., 2018; Varoquaux et al., 2019; 
Xie and Xu, 2019 ). Sorghum holds significant value both in agriculture 
and industry, and it is an outstanding salt and alkali tolerant crop (Gao 
et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2022 ). Therefore, identifying 
the genes and mechanisms regulating salt tolerance will help to cultivate 
other salt tolerant crops through genetic engineering, thus enhancing 
the comprehensive utilization of saline-alkali lands.
In this study, we identified a WRKY gene SbWRKY51 from sorghum 
transcriptome analysis, which was up-regulated under salt stress, 
drought, and ABA treatments. SbWRKY51 -overexpressed sorghum and 
Arabidopsis transgenic plants both showed enhanced salt tolerance. 
Meanwhile, the Arabidopsis homologous gene mutant wrky11 of 
SbWRKY51 exhibited reduced salt tolerance. Quantitative real-time PCR 
(qRT-PCR) analyses indicated that the overexpression of SbWRKY51 led 
to significant changes in the expression of genes related to oxidative 
stress and lignin synthesis in sorghum. In summary, our results sug-
gested that SbWRKY51 was a positive regulator of salt tolerance in 
sorghum.
2.Materials and methods
2.1. Identification and phylogenetic analysis of WRKY family members in 
sorghum
The amino acid sequences of sorghum WRKY proteins were down -
loaded from Ensembl Plants (https://plants.ensembl.org/index.html ). 
We used the WRKY domain ID (PF03106) to identify putative WRKY 
proteins from sorghum protein sequences through TBtools-II (Chen 
et al., 2023 ). We use Batch CD-search (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/ 
Structure/bwrpsb/bwrpsb.cgi ) and SMART (https://smart.embl.de ) to 
remove proteins without WRKY domain(s). The final identification and 
nomenclature of WRKY family members were established with reference 
to Baillo et al. (2020) .
All identified SbWRKY protein sequences were aligned with Clus-
talW and a phylogenetic tree was generated using MEGA7 software. We 
obtained the conserved domain information of all identified SbWRKY 
proteins from NCBI-CDD, and then combined with their amino acid se-
quences, we could visualize the conserved domains using TBtools-II.
2.2. Plant materials and growth conditions
The Arabidopsis T-DNA insertion mutant wrky11 (SALK_141511C) was obtained from the Arabidopsis Biological Resource Center (ABRC). 
The WT, mutants, overexpression, and complementary seeds were kept 
in darkness for two days at 4•C and then sterilized and sowed on Mur-
ashige & Skoog (MS 1) medium [4.43 g/L MS, 30 g/L sucrose, 8.0 g/L 
agar, pH 5.8] (Ma et al., 2012; Xu et al., 2018 ). All lines were grown at 
22•C, with a 16 h light/8 h dark photoperiod.
The sorghum inbred line M-81E was used for SbWRKY51 gene 
amplification, the sorghum line BTx623 was used for Agrobacterium 
rhizogenes -mediated hairy root transformation. Sorghum seeds were 
placed in fine mesh cloth and tied to a water pipe for rinsing for about 
24 h. Evenly spread two layers of filter paper in a round dish and place 
the seeds in the dish for cultivation. When the seeds germinate and have 
long roots, transfer them into hydroponic boxes for continued cultiva -
tion. Replace Hoagland nutrient solution every day and provide suffi-
cient oxygen to the roots with an oxygen pump. Cultivate until the three- 
leaf and one-heart stage.
2.3. Bioinformatics analysis
The CDS sequence and amino acid sequence of SbWRKY51 were 
obtained from the National Center for Biotechnology Information 
(NCBI) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/ ) and Ensembl Plants 
(https://plants.ensembl.org/ ). Ensembl Plants, SMART (https://smart. 
embl.de ), NCBI, DNAMAN software and MEGA7 software were used 
for multiple sequence alignment, conserved domain analysis, and 
phylogenetic tree analysis.
2.4. Quantitative real-time RT-PCR
Total RNA was extracted from 0.1 g of Arabidopsis or sorghum leaves 
using TRIzol Reagent (TransGen Biotech, China). cDNA was reverse 
transcribed from total RNA using Reverse Transcriptase Evo M-MLV RT 
kit. The qRT-PCR was performed with the following program: 95•C for 
30 s, followed by 40 cycles of 95•C for 7 s, 60•C for 20 s, and 72•C for 
10 s. The data set was normalized using Sbactin as an internal control. 
The relative expression of each gene was calculated using the 2 ΔΔCT 
method (Livak and Schmittgen, 2001 ). The primers used for qRT-PCR 
are described in Table S2.
2.5. Expression pattern of SbWRKY51 in sorghum
At the three-leaf one-heart stage, sorghum seedlings were treated 
with Hoagland nutrient solution containing 150 mM NaCl, 150 mM 
mannitol, or 150 μM ABA for 0, 3, 6, 12, 24, and 48 h, respectively. 
Then, 0.1 g of roots from each group was collected for RNA extraction to 
determine the relative expression level of SbWRKY51 .

2.7. Acquisition of transgenic Arabidopsis plants
The overexpression vector pCAMBIA1300 –35S:SbWRKY51 was

2.8. Acquisition of SbWRKY51-overexpressed sorghum lines through 
Agrobacterium rhizogenes-mediated hairy root transformation
Sorghum BTx623 seeds with uniform size were sterilized and sown 
on MS1 medium, which were placed in the dark for 24 h. When the seeds 
showed tender green bud tips of about 4–5 mm, they were ready to be 
infected. The constructed vector pCAMBIA2300 –35S:SbWRKY51 was 
transformed into the Agrobacterium K599, which then infected the bud 
tip explants. The transformed bud tips were transferred to MS2 medium 
(MS 100–300 mg/L Timentin), which were cultivated in the dark for 
24 h and then in normal condition for 14 days. We detected GFP fluo-
rescence signal, and selected non-contaminated hairy roots, which were 
transplanted to nutrient soil and cultivated for 14 days. Overexpression 
plants were screened by PCR and qRT-PCR assays. Treat each sorghum 
line with 150 mM NaCl for 7 days and then measure the physiological 
indexes.
2.9. Determination of physiological indexes in Arabidopsis and Sorghum
The seeds of different Arabidopsis lines were sown on MS containing 
different salt concentrations, which were cultivated under normal con-
dition. After 24 h, their germination rates were counted every day until 
the 5th day. The main root lengths were photographed and measured in 
the 7th day. The 3-week-old Arabidopsis seedlings in nutrient soil were 
independently treated with 0 mM NaCl and 100 mM NaCl solution for 7 
days. The fresh weight, dry weight, Nacontent, Kcontent, CAT ac-
tivity, and SOD activity were determined. The Naand Kcontents were 
determined according to Song et al. (2020) . NBT and DAB staining were 
also performed according to Song et al. (2022) . Approximately 0.2 g of 
leaves were grinded with 1.6 mL of 50 mM phosphate buffer (pH 7.8). 
After centrifugation at 12000 rpm for 20 min at 4 ℃, the obtained su-
pernatant was the crude enzyme solution. CAT and SOD activities were 
determined as described by Hou et al. (2015) .
About four-week-old transgenic sorghum plants and WT were treated 
with 150 mM NaCl for 7 days, and the related physiological indexes 
were determined.
2.10. DAP-seq analysis
The DAP-seq assay was performed by Bluescape Hebei Biotech 
following the method described by Bartlett et al. (2017) . Briefly, the 
genomic DNA of sorghum inbred M-81E was extracted using CTAB 
method, and then was fragmented into approximately 200-bp fragments 
to construct the DNA library. The CDS of the SbWRKY51 was inserted 
into a pF3K WG (BYDV) Flexi ® Vector (Promega, USA, L5681). The 
resulting fusion protein was expressed using the TNT ® SP6 Coupled 
Wheat Germ Extract System (Promega, USA, L4130). The purified pro-
tein was incubated with the genomic DNA library. The binding DNA 
fragments were eluted and sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq platform 
by Bluescape Hebei Biotech.2.11. Determination of lignin content
The phenolic hydroxyl groups in lignin undergo acetylation and 
exhibit a characteristic absorption peak at 280 nm. The absorbance 
value at 280 nm is positively correlated with the lignin content. The 
determination of lignin content was carried out using a lignin content 
assay kit purchased from Solarbio (Beijing, China).
3.Results
3.1. SbWRKY51 encodes a typical WRKY family protein
Through bioinformatics analysis, we have identified 94 putative 
members of the sorghum WRKY transcription factor family, and con-
structed the phylogenetic tree based on multiple amino acid sequence 
alignment (Figure S1). Each member contains at least one WRKY domain 
(Figure S2). The SbWRKY51 gene contains 948 bases and encodes 315 
amino acids. Amino acid sequence analysis revealed that SbWRKY51 
protein has a typical WRKY domain located between amino acids 230 
and 290, containing a WRKYGQK motif and a C2H2 zinc finger struc-
ture. Aligning the SbWRKY51 protein sequence with homologous pro-
teins of other species revealed that the WRKY domain is conserved in the 
WRKY family (Fig. 1A). Thus, SbWRKY51 is a typical group II WRKY 
protein.
The phylogenetic tree analysis showed that the SbWRKY51 protein is 
highly homologous to ZmWRKY51 in Zea mays, PaWRKY51 in Panicum 
hallii, OsWRKY51 in Oryza sativa . In addition, SbWRKY51 also showed 
high homology with AtWRKY11 in the model plant Arabidopsis thaliana . 
The conserved domain analysis of these proteins revealed that they all 
have the same conserved WRKY domain (Fig. 1B).
3.2. SbWRKY51 is a salt stress-responsive transcription factor
To investigate the function of SbWRKY51 in response to different 
abiotic stresses, we detected the expression levels of SbWRKY51 in 
sorghum inbred line M-81E under salt, mannitol, or ABA treatments. 
qRT-PCR analysis showed that the expression of SbWRKY51 was 
generally upregulated under different times of salt treatment. Similarly, 
different times of mannitol or ABA treatment also generally upregulated 
the expression of SbWRKY51 (Fig. 2A).
Most transcription factors are located in the nucleus, to determine 
the subcellular localization of SbWRKY51, the coding region of 
SbWRKY51 was fused to the green fluorescent protein (GFP) gene under 
the control of the CaMV 35S promoter. This recombinant plasmid and 
the GFP vector were separately transfected into the tobacco leaves. The 
green fluorescence of SbWRKY51-GFP was only observed in the nucleus, 
while the signals of GFP alone were observed in plasma membranes and 
nucleus (Fig. 2B), indicating that SbWRKY51 is a nucleus-localized 
protein.
Besides, to determine whether SbWRKY51 has transcriptional ac-
tivity, the constructed pGBKT7- SbWRKY51 vector and pGBKT7 vector 
alone were separately transformed into yeast cells. The results showed 
that the transformants containing the pGBKT7- SbWRKY51 vector and 
pGBKT7 vector both could grow on the SD/-Trp medium, but only 
transformants containing the pGBKT7- SbWRKY51 vector could grow on 
the SD/-Trp-His-Ade medium and turn blue on the SD/-Trp-His-Ade 
X-α-gal medium (Fig. 2C). This result indicated that SbWRKY51 had 
transcriptional activation activity in yeast cells.

3.4. SbWRKY51 positively regulates salt tolerance of sorghum
To investigate whether this gene has the same function in regulating 
salt tolerance in sorghum as it does in Arabidopsis , we overexpressed 
SbWRKY51 under the control of the ubiquitin promoter in BTx623 (a 
sorghum inbred) background through Agrobacterium rhizogenes -medi -
ated hairy root transformation. OE3 and OE8 were screened out as 
representative overexpressing lines through fluorescence level identifi -
cation, PCR, and qRT-PCR analyses (Figure S2). After 7 days of 150mM 
NaCl treatment, the SbWRKY51 -overexpressed transgenic lines grew 
better and taller than the WT plants (Fig. 5A), with longer root length 
(Fig. 5B) and more aboveground and underground biomass (Fig. 5C, D). 
We also measured the Naand Kcontents with or without NaCl 
treatment. The results showed that the SbWRKY51 -overexpressed lines 
absorbed less Naand more K, with a lower Na/Kratio compared 
with those of WT under 150mM NaCl treatment (Fig. 5E-G). The above results indicated that overexpression of SbWRKY51 enhanced the 
tolerance of sorghum to salt stress.

4.Discussion
Sorghum possesses excellent salt tolerant ability, so exploring its salt- 
tolerant genes and the underlying mechanism of salt tolerance is of great 
theoretical value for breeding other salt-tolerant crops. Accumulating 
evidence has shown that WRKY proteins play important roles in biotic 
and abiotic stress responses in various species (Gong et al., 2015; Cai 
et al., 2017; Bao et al., 2018; Ye et al., 2021; Zhu et al., 2021 ). However, 
only several WRKY genes have been reported to be involved in sorghum 
salt tolerance until now. Thus, exploring the underlying mechanism of 
sorghum WRKY genes in plant response to salt stress is of great signifi -
cance. In this study, we identified a novel sorghum WRKY gene SbWRKY51 , whose expression was induced by several abiotic stresses 
including salt stress (Fig. 2A). Multiple sequence alignment and protein 
domain analysis showed that SbWRKY51 belongs to the group II WRKY 
family (Fig. 1). Phylogenetic analysis indicated that SbWRKY51 showed 
high similarity to AtWRKY11 in Arabidopsis (Fig. 1B). SbWRKY51 was 
located in nucleus and showed transcriptional activation activity in 
yeast cells (Fig. 2B, C). Therefore, these results suggested that 
SbWRKY51 might function as a group II WRKY transcription factor 
involved in salt stress response in plants. In this study, SbWRKY51 -o-
verexpressed sorghum and ectopic expression Arabidopsis , as well as 
wrkt11 mutant and complementary lines were used for salt tolerance 
assays. In this study, under salt stress treatment, the Arabidopsis over-
expression lines showed higher germination rate, longer root length, 
higher biomass than those of WT and complementary lines, and these 
physiological indexes in the mutant was the lowest (Figs. 3, 4A-B). 
Similarly, the sorghum overexpression lines also displayed longer root 
length and higher biomass than those of WT (Fig. 5A-D). These results 
indicated that SbWRKY51 might play a positive role in plant response to 
salt stress. Salt stress usually disturbs Na/Khomeostasis in plants, and 
Na/Kratio is an important indicator for the salt tolerance of plants. In 
this study, the SbWRKY51 -overexpressed Arabidopsis and sorghum 
transgenic lines displayed lower Na/Kratio than that in WT plants 
(Figs. 4C-E, 5E-G), indicating that SbWRKY51 -overexpressed plants kept 
relatively strong ability to maintain Na/Khomeostasis in cells, 
thereby enhancing plant salt tolerance.

et al., 2024 ). A moderate amount of ROS can act as a signaling molecule 
to trigger the stress responses in plants (Suzuki et al., 2011; Lodeyro 
et al., 2016 ). However, excessive ROS can damage the structure and 
function of plant cells, causing oxidative damage to plants. In this study, 
the NBT and DAB staining showed that the SbWRKY51 -overexpressed 
Arabidopsis transgenic lines accumulated less ROS than the WT plants 
under salt stress (Fig. 4F, G). To quench excessive ROS, plants have an 
antioxidant enzyme system consisting of multiple ROS-scavenging en-
zymes, such as CAT and SOD. In this study, the SbWRKY51 -overex -
pressed Arabidopsis and sorghum transgenic plants showed higher CAT 
and SOD activities than those in the WT plants (Figs. 4H-I, 6B-C). 
Moreover, our study also revealed that, under salt stress, the expression 
of ROS-scavenging genes, like APX, CAT and GPX6 , were up-regulated in 
the sorghum overexpression lines, while the expression of 
ROS-producing gene RBOHA was significantly down-regulated 
compared with the WT plants (Fig. 6A). Furthermore, the GO enrich -
ment analysis of DAP-seq also revealed that the target genes of 
SbWRKY51 were highly enriched in the response to oxidative stress 
process (Figure S4A). Taken together, these results suggested that 
SbWRKY51 might play an important role in regulating cellular ROS 
levels, and therefore participate in oxidative stress.
The GO and KEGG enrichment analyses revealed that the target 
genes of SbWRKY51 were enriched in plant-type cell wall and phenyl -
propanoid biosynthesis pathway (Figure S4). Lignin is an important 
component of plant cell wall, and the phenylpropanoid biosynthesis 
pathway provides monomers for the biosynthesis of lignin (Liu et al., 
2015; Dong and Lin, 2021 ). Moreover, many studies have reported that 
lignin is involved in the salt stress response of plants (Guo et al., 2016; Hu et al., 2019; Duan et al., 2020; Dabravolski and Isayenkov, 2023 ). 
Salt stress can lead to cell dehydration, the deposition of lignin in the 
secondary cell wall can enhance the mechanical support of the cell wall 
and maintain tissue morphology (Liu et al., 2018; Muro-Villanueva 
et al., 2019 ). Cell wall lignification can limit the diffusion of Nainto 
the cells and reduce ionic toxicity. The lignification of the root endo-
dermis (such as the Casparian strip) forms a selective barrier that pre-
vents Nafrom entering the vascular bundles through the apoplast 
pathway (Dabravolski and Isayenkov, 2023; Yadav and Chattopadhyay, 
2023 ). Moreover, salt stress can up-regulate the gene expression of key 
enzymes in the phenylpropanoid pathway (such as PAL, C4H), pro-
moting lignin synthesis (Shafi et al., 2015; Ma and Constabel, 2019; 
Torres et al., 2020; Kim et al., 2022 ). In this study, we analyzed the 
expression levels of genes coding for key enzymes directly related to 
lignin synthesis in the phenylpropanoid pathway, including SbPAL , 
SbC4H , SbCCR1 , SbC3H , SbF5H , and SbCOMT . The results showed that, 
under salt stress, the expression of these lignin biosynthesis-related 
genes in SbWRKY51 -overexpressed sorghum lines were significantly 
up-regulated compared to WT (Fig. 6D). Moreover, the lignin content in 
the sorghum overexpression lines was also significantly higher than that 
in WT plants (Fig. 6E). Therefore, SbWRKY51 might improve plant salt 
tolerance by regulating ROS levels and influencing lignin synthesis.
Taken together, under salt stress, the overexpression of SbWRKY51 
in sorghum increased the activities of antioxidant enzymes and 
improved the expression levels of ROS-scavenging related genes and 
lignin biosynthesis-related genes, as well as the lignin contents. Our 
research indicates that SbWRKY51 positively regulates the salt tolerance 
of sorghum. However, whether SbWRKY51 plays a direct positive role in 
regulating ROS levels and lignin biosynthesis still needs further inves -
tigation in the future.
